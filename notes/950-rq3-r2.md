## What two rounds cannot show

- **A rate, an interval, or an effect size.** Two observations of a pair give a count of flips and nothing more. 16 of 144 pairs disagreed between the rounds; that number is not a per-pair flip probability, no confidence interval is computed from it, and none should be quoted from this report.
- **Which of the two rounds is the outlier.** Where a cell moved by 2 tasks, nothing here says whether round one or round two is the unusual one. That needs more rounds per cell, not two.
- **A clean separation of run-to-run variance from everything else.** The runner commit differs between the rounds, the two provider models carry no version identifier and could have been updated silently between them, and one round-two run never reached a verdict at all. A flip therefore cannot be attributed to sampling alone.
- **Anything about tools, or about cost.** Every cell of both rounds is the no-tools arm. The seconds column of the per-cell table is a cost figure and not a cost claim; the wall-time reading of round one is not re-made here.
- **More of the task set than round one could use.** `hello-go` passed in all 18 cells again (`round-two-hello-go-passes-over-the-eighteen-cells`) and `split-module-python` in 6 of them again (`round-two-split-module-python-passes-over-the-eighteen-cells`), so a ceiling and a floor still contribute nothing and the comparison rests on the rest of the set.
- **A net energy figure for two of the local cells.** The idle-baseline wraparound hit a different pair of windows this time, and the affected cells are reported as not measured rather than filled in.
